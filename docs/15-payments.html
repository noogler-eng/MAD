Payments — In-App Purchases, Stripe aur Security

1. Payment Basics — The Two Categories

Sabse pehle ek fundamental mental model clear karte hain jo har mobile dev ko pata hona chahiye — chahe wo kabhi Stripe ya RevenueCat na bhi chhue, ye distinction unke career mein kabhi na kabhi matter karega. Mobile payments do bilkul alag categories mein baante jaate hain, aur dono ke liye alag rules aur alag payment providers use karne padte hain.

Category A — Digital goods/services jo app ke andar consume hote hain (In-App Purchases)

Ye woh cheezein hain jo user app ke andar hi use karta hai — premium features unlock karna, ek subscription (jaise ad-free experience ya extra storage), virtual currency/coins, ek game mein extra lives, cloud storage upgrade, waghera. Apple aur Google ke policies legally require karte hain ki in cheezon ke liye tumhe unka apna In-App Purchase system use karna hi padega — iOS pe StoreKit, Android pe Google Play Billing. Tum apna Stripe ya koi bhi external payment gateway digital goods ke liye use nahi kar sakte — ye ek App Store/Play Store policy violation hai, aur violate karne pe app reject ho sakta hai ya already live app remove ho sakta hai.

Category B — Real-world goods/services (physical ya real-world value)

Ye woh cheezein hain jinki value app ke bahar real duniya mein deliver hoti hai — physical products (jaise Amazon pe kuch order karna), ride-hailing (Uber ek ride book kar raha hai), food delivery (Zomato/Swiggy khana deliver kar rahe hain), ya real-world services (ek plumber book karna, ek hotel room book karna). In cases mein Apple/Google ka 15-30% commission rule apply nahi hota — tum free ho Stripe, PayPal, Razorpay, ya kisi bhi payment processor ko directly use karne ke liye.

🚨 Security/Policy Critical — Category Galat Samajhna App Reject Karva Sakta Hai

Ye distinction itna important hai ki Apple/Google ke review teams specifically isi cheez ko check karte hain. Concrete examples jo senior-level business/technical decisions ban chuke hain:

  • Netflix aur Spotify iOS pe in-app subscribe nahi karne dete — ye accident nahi hai. Digital subscription (streaming access) technically Category A (digital good) maana jaata hai, matlab Apple ka 30% cut lagta agar wo StoreKit se subscribe karwate. Business decision: dono companies ne apne app se in-app purchase flow hi hata diya, aur users ko bahar (web browser) pe bhej ke apni website se subscribe karwate hain — isse Apple ka commission completely avoid ho jaata hai. Ye "External Purchase Link" pattern hai jo Apple ne (regulatory pressure ke baad, especially EU mein) allow kiya hai limited cases mein — lekin ye seedha in-app purchase se technically/legally alag treatment hai.
  • Apps reject hone ka common real-world reason — koi app jo premium feature ya subscription unlock karne ke liye apna custom Stripe checkout screen app ke andar dikhata hai (bina StoreKit/Play Billing ke), Apple review mein almost guaranteed reject hota hai under Guideline 3.1.1 (In-App Purchase). Ye ek bahut common first-time-founder mistake hai — "Stripe seedha integrate kar dete hain, RevenueCat/StoreKit complex lagta hai" — aur phir app store review mein reject ho jaata hai.
  • Uber/Zomato/Swiggy apne payment ke liye Stripe/Razorpay/apna gateway use kar sakte hain bina kisi policy violation ke, kyunki ride ya khana ek real-world service/good hai — Category B. Yehi wajah hai ki tumne kabhi Uber mein "In-App Purchase" ka Apple/Google confirmation dialog nahi dekha hoga jab tum ride ke liye pay karte ho.

Rule of thumb jo yaad rakhna: "Kya user isse app ke andar consume kar raha hai (digital) ya real duniya mein (physical/service)?" — pehla case StoreKit/Play Billing (RevenueCat se wrap karke), doosra case Stripe/koi bhi processor directly.

2. In-App Purchases with RevenueCat

Ab jab humne establish kar liya ki digital goods ke liye StoreKit/Play Billing mandatory hai, agla sawaal — inhe raw use karna kitna painful hai? Bahut. StoreKit aur Play Billing dono bilkul alag APIs hain (Swift/Kotlin-level native concepts), receipt validation ka apna alag format hai har platform pe, subscription status track karna (active/expired/grace period/refunded) manually implement karna padta hai, aur "restore purchases" flow khud se sahi banana bhi non-trivial hai. Yehi wajah hai RevenueCat jaisi service industry standard ban gayi hai.

RevenueCat kya solve karta hai

  • Ek unified API — iOS StoreKit aur Android Play Billing dono ko ek hi JS/TS SDK (react-native-purchases) ke peeche wrap kar deta hai, matlab tumhe platform-specific purchase code alag se nahi likhna padta.
  • Server-side receipt validation — RevenueCat ka backend khud Apple/Google ke servers se receipt verify karta hai (section 3 mein detail), tumhe khud ye complex, error-prone logic likhne ki zaroorat nahi.
  • Subscription status sync — RevenueCat "entitlements" ka concept deta hai (jaise "premium") jo automatically active/inactive reflect karta hai, chahe user ne renew kiya ho, cancel kiya ho, ya refund liya ho — bina tumhe har state manually track kiye.
  • Cross-platform entitlement management — agar user ne iOS pe subscribe kiya aur phir Android device pe same account se login kiya, RevenueCat dono platforms ke purchases ko ek hi user identity ke against merge kar sakta hai (via appUserID).
🧠 Senior Dev Note — Raw StoreKit/Billing kyun avoid karte hain

Raw StoreKit integration mein receipt validation ke liye tumhe khud Apple ke verification server (verifyReceipt ya naya App Store Server API) ko hit karna padta hai, response parse karna padta hai, subscription renewal/expiry dates khud calculate karne padte hain, aur "restore purchases" flow manually implement karna padta hai jisme edge cases bahut hain (jaise user ne app delete karke reinstall kiya, ya naya device liya). Ye itna error-prone hai ki bade tech companies bhi is problem ko khud solve karne ke bajaye RevenueCat jaisi managed service use karna prefer karti hain — "build vs buy" ka classic example hai jahan "buy" almost hamesha sahi decision hai.

Setup — packages fetch karna aur purchase karna

Poora flow dekhte hain: SDK configure karna, available packages (jaise "Monthly", "Annual" plans) fetch karna, ek purchase initiate karna, aur entitlement check karna.

npx expo install react-native-purchases # RevenueCat ka official React Native SDK, expo install version-compatibility resolve karta hai
lib/purchases.ts
import Purchases, { LOG_LEVEL } from "react-native-purchases"; // RevenueCat SDK ka default export aur log-level enum import kiya
import { Platform } from "react-native"; // platform check karne ke liye (iOS/Android alag API keys chahiye)

export function configurePurchases() {
  // production mein verbose logs off rakho, dev build mein debug ke liye ON kar sakte ho
  Purchases.setLogLevel(__DEV__ ? LOG_LEVEL.DEBUG : LOG_LEVEL.ERROR); // __DEV__ RN ka built-in global hai jo dev vs production build batata hai

  const apiKey = Platform.select({ // har platform ka RevenueCat public SDK key alag hota hai
    ios: process.env.EXPO_PUBLIC_REVENUECAT_IOS_KEY, // iOS-specific public key, RevenueCat dashboard se milti hai
    android: process.env.EXPO_PUBLIC_REVENUECAT_ANDROID_KEY, // Android-specific public key, alag hoti hai iOS se
  });

  if (!apiKey) { // safety check — agar key missing hai (misconfigured env), fail fast karo
    throw new Error("RevenueCat API key missing"); // clear error taaki dev ko turant pata chale, silent fail nahi
  }

  Purchases.configure({ apiKey }); // SDK ko is public key se initialize kiya — ye app start hote hi ek baar call hota hai
}
ℹ️ Ye "public" API key kyun safe hai

RevenueCat ki apiKey yahan client bundle mein hai aur EXPO_PUBLIC_ prefix ke saath — ye theek hai kyunki ye ek public SDK identifier hai (ch.9 ke Supabase anon key jaisa concept), jiska kaam sirf ye batana hai "ye request kis RevenueCat project se aa rahi hai". Actual purchase security Apple/Google ke apne systems se aati hai, is key se nahi. RevenueCat ka secret API key (server-to-server REST API ke liye) alag hoti hai aur wo sirf backend pe honi chahiye — kabhi client mein nahi (ch.9 ka wahi client-vs-server key pattern).

Packages fetch karna aur display karna

RevenueCat mein "Offerings" ka concept hai — ek offering ke andar multiple "packages" ho sakte hain (jaise Monthly, Annual). Ye configuration RevenueCat dashboard se aati hai, hardcode nahi karte — isse pricing/plans change karne ke liye app update ki zaroorat nahi padti.

hooks/usePurchases.ts
import { useEffect, useState } from "react"; // state aur lifecycle hooks
import Purchases, { type PurchasesPackage, type CustomerInfo } from "react-native-purchases"; // SDK aur uske TS types

export function usePurchases() {
  const [packages, setPackages] = useState<PurchasesPackage[]>([]); // available plans (Monthly/Annual) ki list
  const [isPremium, setIsPremium] = useState(false); // current user ka premium entitlement status
  const [loading, setLoading] = useState(true); // initial fetch complete hua ya nahi

  // customer info se entitlement status derive karne wala helper
  const syncEntitlement = (info: CustomerInfo) => {
    const active = typeof info.entitlements.active["premium"] !== "undefined"; // "premium" naam ka entitlement RevenueCat dashboard mein define hota hai
    setIsPremium(active); // state update — UI turant is naye status ke hisaab se re-render hoga
  };

  useEffect(() => {
    async function load() {
      try {
        const offerings = await Purchases.getOfferings(); // RevenueCat dashboard se configured offerings fetch kiye
        if (offerings.current) { // "current" offering wo hai jo dashboard mein active/default set hai
          setPackages(offerings.current.availablePackages); // us offering ke andar ke packages (plans) state mein daale
        }

        const customerInfo = await Purchases.getCustomerInfo(); // current logged-in RevenueCat user ka purchase/entitlement info fetch kiya
        syncEntitlement(customerInfo); // premium status turant reflect karo, purani cached value pe depend mat karo
      } catch (err) {
        console.error("RevenueCat load failed", err); // network/config error ko log karo, app crash nahi hona chahiye
      } finally {
        setLoading(false); // success ho ya fail, loading state hamesha off karo
      }
    }

    load(); // component mount hote hi offerings + entitlement fetch karo

    // RevenueCat listener — subscription status kahin bhi background mein change ho (jaise renewal), UI auto-sync ho jaaye
    const listener = Purchases.addCustomerInfoUpdateListener(syncEntitlement); // har baar customerInfo update ho, syncEntitlement dobara chalega

    return () => {
      Purchases.removeCustomerInfoUpdateListener(listener); // cleanup — component unmount pe listener hata do, memory leak/dangling callback se bachne ke liye
    };
  }, []); // empty deps — sirf mount pe setup, unmount pe cleanup

  // ---------- PURCHASE ----------
  const purchasePackage = async (pkg: PurchasesPackage) => {
    try {
      const { customerInfo } = await Purchases.purchasePackage(pkg); // ye StoreKit/Play Billing ka native purchase dialog trigger karta hai
      syncEntitlement(customerInfo); // purchase complete hote hi entitlement state turant update karo
      return true; // caller ko success signal diya
    } catch (err: any) {
      if (!err.userCancelled) { // user ne khud dialog cancel kiya toh error dikhana zaroori nahi
        console.error("Purchase failed", err); // genuine failure (network, declined card, etc.) ko log karo
      }
      return false; // caller ko failure signal diya
    }
  };

  // ---------- RESTORE ----------
  const restorePurchases = async () => {
    const customerInfo = await Purchases.restorePurchases(); // Apple/Google account history se pichle purchases wapas link karta hai
    syncEntitlement(customerInfo); // agar koi valid purchase mila, entitlement state turant reflect ho
  };

  return { packages, isPremium, loading, purchasePackage, restorePurchases }; // hook ka public interface — component isse consume karega
}

3. Receipt Validation — Why It Matters

Ab ek subtle lekin bahut critical security concept — jab StoreKit/Play Billing kehta hai "purchase successful", iska matlab ye proof nahi hai ki payment genuinely complete hua. Ye samajhna zaroori hai kyunki naive implementation is gap ko exploit-able bana deta hai.

Problem — client-side "success" event trust nahi kiya ja sakta

Jab tum purchasePackage() call karte ho aur wo resolve ho jaata hai bina error ke, tumhara instinct hoga "great, ab premium unlock kar dete hain client state mein hi". Lekin socho — ye event client device pe fire ho raha hai, jismein tumhare control se bahar bahut cheezein possible hain:

  • Ek jailbroken/rooted device pe tools exist karte hain (jaise Lucky Patcher, ya custom jailbreak tweaks) jo fake purchase success responses inject kar sakte hain bina actual payment kiye — StoreKit/Billing ka response app ko lagta hai genuine, lekin actually intercept/fake kiya gaya hai.
  • Network traffic intercept/modify karke (proxy tools se) response ko tamper kiya ja sakta hai agar verification sirf client-side ho rahi hai aur server kabhi involve hi nahi hota.
  • Simplest attack: agar tumhara app ka logic sirf "purchase succeeded" boolean pe depend karta hai bina kisi cryptographic receipt check ke, koi bhi reverse-engineered build ya modified APK is boolean ko directly true set kar sakta hai.

Solution — receipt validation Apple/Google ke servers ke against

Har purchase ke saath ek receipt (iOS) ya purchase token (Android) generate hota hai, jo Apple/Google ne cryptographically sign kiya hota hai. Ye receipt Apple/Google ke apne verification servers ko bhej ke confirm karwaya ja sakta hai ki ye genuine hai aur actually valid purchase represent karta hai. RevenueCat ye pura kaam apne backend pe automatically karta hai — jab purchasePackage() resolve hota hai, RevenueCat ka server-side ne already receipt ko Apple/ Google se verify kar liya hota hai before entitlement ko "active" mark karne se pehle.

🚨 Security Critical — Kabhi Bhi Client-Side Boolean Pe Purely Trust Mat Karo

High-value features (jaise ek paid subscription jo real revenue represent karta hai, ya ek in-app currency jo real money se bought hui hai) ke liye kabhi bhi sirf ek client-side flag (jaise AsyncStorage mein { isPremium: true }) pe depend mat karo bina server-side verification ke. Sahi pattern:

  • RevenueCat/StoreKit/Billing use kar rahe ho toh — RevenueCat ke CustomerInfo.entitlements ko source of truth maano, jo already server-verified hai (jaisa upar discuss kiya).
  • Agar tumhara khud ka backend hai jo premium content serve karta hai (jaise ek API jo premium-only data return karta hai), toh us backend ko khud RevenueCat/App Store Server API/webhook se entitlement status check karna chahiye — client se aaya "mai premium hoon" claim kabhi bhi backend ko trust nahi karna chahiye bina independent verification ke.
  • Ek common mistake: sirf client mein entitlement check karke UI hide kar dena (jaise ek premium button ko conditionally render karna), lekin actual data/API endpoint backend mein bina entitlement check ke expose rehna. Ye bilkul waisa hi security gap hai jaisa ch.9 mein RLS missing hone se hota hai — client-side UI hiding koi real security nahi hai, sirf UX hai.

4. Stripe for Real-World Payments

Ab Category B (real-world goods/services) ke liye Stripe ka standard, secure integration pattern dekhte hain. Ye pattern ch.9 ke BFF (Backend-for-Frontend) concept ka direct extension hai — wahi mental model, bas Stripe ke context mein.

Kyun ek backend zaroori hai (client kabhi seedha PaymentIntent nahi bana sakta)

Stripe ke do tarah ke API keys hote hain — publishable key (pk_live_..., client mein safe) aur secret key (sk_live_..., sirf server-side). Ek payment process karne ke liye pehle ek PaymentIntent object banana padta hai Stripe ke saath — is object creation ke liye secret key chahiye hoti hai, kyunki isi step pe amount, currency, aur customer details set hote hain jo agar client control kar sake toh attacker apna amount khud set kar sakta hai (jaise ₹10,000 ka order ₹1 mein pay karne ki koshish).

Standard secure flow (exactly ch.9 ke API-key-separation model jaisa hai):

  1. Client apne backend (Expo Router API route ya alag server) ko request bhejta hai — "mujhe ye order pay karna hai".
  2. Backend, Stripe secret key use karke, ek PaymentIntent banata hai with the correct server-computed amount (client se bheja gaya amount trust nahi karte — backend khud order/cart se amount calculate karta hai).
  3. Backend client ko sirf ek client_secret string wapas bhejta hai — ye ek limited-scope token hai jo sirf isi specific PaymentIntent ko confirm karne ke liye use ho sakta hai.
  4. Client, publishable key ke saath, @stripe/stripe-react-native use karke us client_secret se payment confirm karta hai (card details enter karke).
app/api/create-payment-intent+api.ts (backend)
import Stripe from "stripe"; // Stripe ka official Node SDK, server-side use ke liye

const stripe = new Stripe(process.env.STRIPE_SECRET_KEY!); // secret key sirf yahan, server env var se — ye file client bundle mein kabhi nahi jaati (+api.ts convention, ch.9)

export async function POST(request: Request) {
  const { orderId } = await request.json(); // client sirf orderId bhejta hai, amount nahi — amount client se kabhi trust nahi karte

  const order = await getOrderFromDatabase(orderId); // apne database se order fetch kiya — real amount yahan se aata hai (helper function, apna DB layer)
  if (!order) { // order exist nahi karta toh aage mat badho
    return Response.json({ error: "Order not found" }, { status: 404 }); // 404 = not found status
  }

  const paymentIntent = await stripe.paymentIntents.create({ // Stripe ke saath naya PaymentIntent object banaya
    amount: order.totalInPaise, // backend-computed amount (paise/cents mein), client se aaya amount nahi use kiya
    currency: "inr", // currency code, lowercase Stripe convention hai
    metadata: { orderId: order.id }, // baad mein webhook mein is payment ko order se link karne ke liye reference
  });

  return Response.json({ clientSecret: paymentIntent.client_secret }); // sirf client_secret return kiya — poora paymentIntent object nahi, sirf jitna client ko chahiye
}
components/CheckoutScreen.tsx (client)
import { useState } from "react"; // local state ke liye
import { View, Button, Alert } from "react-native"; // UI primitives
import { CardField, useStripe } from "@stripe/stripe-react-native"; // Stripe ka RN SDK — CardField ek pre-built, PCI-safe card input component hai (section 5 mein detail)

function CheckoutScreen({ orderId }: { orderId: string }) {
  const { confirmPayment } = useStripe(); // Stripe SDK ka hook, publishable key se already configured (app root mein StripeProvider ke through)
  const [loading, setLoading] = useState(false); // payment process ho raha hai ya nahi, UI disable karne ke liye

  const handlePay = async () => {
    setLoading(true); // button disable/spinner dikhana shuru

    const res = await fetch("/api/create-payment-intent", { // apne backend ko call kiya, seedha Stripe ko nahi
      method: "POST", // POST request kyunki data create ho raha hai
      headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" }, // JSON body bhej rahe hain
      body: JSON.stringify({ orderId }), // sirf orderId bheja, amount nahi (amount backend khud nikaalega)
    });
    const { clientSecret } = await res.json(); // backend se mila client_secret parse kiya

    const { error } = await confirmPayment(clientSecret, { // is client_secret se payment confirm kiya, card details CardField se automatically liye jaate hain
      paymentMethodType: "Card", // payment method type specify kiya
    });

    setLoading(false); // process complete, loading state off

    if (error) { // payment fail hua (declined card, insufficient funds, etc.)
      Alert.alert("Payment failed", error.message); // user ko readable error dikhaya
    } else {
      Alert.alert("Success", "Payment complete!"); // payment successful — real confirmation backend webhook se bhi aayega (section 7)
    }
  };

  return (
    <View>
      <CardField
        postalCodeEnabled={false} {/* India ke liye postal code field ki zaroorat nahi */}
        style={{ height: 50 }} {/* Stripe ka native, PCI-safe card input — raw card number kabhi humare code mein nahi aata */}
      />
      <Button title={loading ? "Processing..." : "Pay"} onPress={handlePay} disabled={loading} />
    </View>
  );
}

export default CheckoutScreen; // component export kiya reuse ke liye
🚨 Security Critical — Stripe SECRET Key Kabhi RN App Mein Nahi

Bilkul ch.9 ke EXPO_PUBLIC_STRIPE_SECRET_KEY danger callout jaisa hi warning yahan bhi repeat kar rahe hain kyunki ye galti bahut common hai — Stripe secret key (sk_live_... ya sk_test_...) kabhi bhi kisi bhi form mein RN app ke bundle mein nahi honi chahiye, chahe env var prefix kuch bhi ho. Agar ye leak ho jaaye, attacker seedha tumhare Stripe account se charges create kar sakta hai, refunds issue kar sakta hai, ya customer payment data access kar sakta hai. Sirf publishable key (pk_live_...) client-safe hai — usi naam se hi clear hai ki ye "publish" karne ke liye design hui hai. Secret key sirf server (+api.ts route jaisa BFF layer) ke environment variables mein rehni chahiye.

5. PCI Compliance — What a Mobile Dev Needs to Know

PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) ek security standard hai jo define karta hai ki card data (number, CVV, expiry) ko kaise handle karna chahiye. Full PCI compliance achieve karna ek bahut heavy process hai — audits, specific infrastructure requirements, ongoing monitoring. Achi khabar ye hai ki ek mobile dev ke roop mein tumhe usually isse directly deal nahi karna padta, agar tum sahi tools use karo.

Tokenization — raw card data tumhare code ko touch hi nahi karta

@stripe/stripe-react-native ka CardField component (jo upar use kiya) is wajah se exist karta hai — jab user card number type karta hai, wo directly Stripe ke native SDK ke andar capture hota hai aur seedha Stripe ke servers ko bheja jaata hai tokenization ke through. Raw card number kabhi tumhare JavaScript code, tumhare state, ya tumhare backend server ko touch hi nahi karta — sirf ek safe, single-use token (jaise upar wala client_secret/payment method reference) tumhare paas aata hai.

Isi wajah se senior devs almost kabhi bhi custom card-number input field khud se nahi banate (jaise ek plain TextInput jisme card number type ho aur tum use apne state mein store karo aur khud apne backend ko bhejo). Agar tum aisa karte ho, raw card data tumhare app/server ke through guzarta hai — jiska matlab tumhara poora app/backend infrastructure ab PCI scope mein aa jaata hai, jisse compliance requirements bahut heavy ho jaate hain (network segmentation, specific encryption standards, regular audits, waghera).

🧠 Senior Dev Note — "SAQ A" scope ka fayda

Jab tum Stripe/similar provider ke pre-built, tokenizing UI components use karte ho aur raw card data kabhi apne servers ko touch nahi karte, tumhara business typically PCI SAQ A (Self-Assessment Questionnaire A) category mein aata hai — ye sabse simple/lightest compliance tier hai, kyunki tumhara infrastructure card data ko kabhi "see" hi nahi karta. Jaise hi tum raw card data khud handle karna shuru karte ho, tum SAQ D jaisi heavy category mein chale jaate ho. Yehi practical reason hai ki "Stripe Elements/CardField use karo, apna card input mat banao" ek universal senior advice hai.

6. Testing Payments

Payments testing ke liye har provider apna sandbox/test environment deta hai — production/real money involve kiye bina poora flow test karne ke liye. In sandboxes ko use karna sirf convenience nahi, ye mandatory practice hai.

ProviderTest mechanismNotes
Apple / StoreKit Sandbox Tester accounts (App Store Connect → Users and Access → Sandbox Testers) Ek dedicated Apple ID banao jo sirf testing ke liye ho, real Apple ID se kabhi sandbox test mat karo. Sandbox mein subscriptions accelerated speed pe renew hoti hain (jaise 1 hafte ki subscription 3 min mein renew ho jaati hai) taaki renewal/expiry flows jaldi test ho sakein.
Google / Play Billing License Testing accounts (Play Console → Setup → License testing) + internal/closed testing tracks License testers real charge nahi lagte, lekin real purchase flow simulate karte hain, including subscription states.
RevenueCat Sandbox mode automatically detect hota hai jab app StoreKit/Play sandbox credentials use kar raha ho RevenueCat dashboard mein sandbox vs production purchases alag se dikhte hain, dono ko confuse mat karo jab metrics dekh rahe ho.
Stripe Test mode API keys (pk_test_.../sk_test_...) + published test card numbers Common test card: 4242 4242 4242 4242 (successful payment), aur specific decline-simulating numbers Stripe docs mein listed hain (jaise insufficient funds, expired card scenarios).
⚠️ Real Payment Methods Se Development Mein Kabhi Test Mat Karo

Ye tempting lag sakta hai "arre bas ek chhota amount se real test kar leta hoon", lekin isse kai problems hoti hain — real charges lagte hain (jinhe refund karna extra kaam hai), accidental repeat charges ho sakte hain agar test loop mein bug ho, aur agar production Stripe/RevenueCat keys galti se dev build mein reh gayi (jo ek common mistake hai), tum khud apne real customer base ke against test transactions create kar sakte ho. Hamesha test/sandbox keys ko dev builds mein aur live keys ko sirf production builds mein use karo — environment-based config (jaise ch.1 ke app.config.ts pattern) se ye separation enforce karo.

7. Real-World Gotchas

⚠️ "Restore Purchases" Bhool Jaana — Common Rejection Reason

Apple ki guidelines explicitly require karti hain ki agar user apna app delete karke reinstall kare, ya naya device pe same Apple ID se login kare, unhe apne pichle non-consumable purchases/subscriptions ko restore karne ka option milna chahiye — bina dobara pay kiye. Ye ek deceptively simple lagne wala requirement hai jo bahut baar developers bhool jaate hain jab MVP jaldi ship karna hota hai, aur ye ek bahut common App Store rejection reason hai. Upar wale usePurchases hook mein restorePurchases() function isi liye hai — UI mein ek visible "Restore Purchases" button hamesha rakho (paywall screen pe, ya settings mein).

⚠️ Subscription States Sirf "Active/Inactive" Nahi Hain

Naya dev sochta hai subscription ek simple boolean hai — active ya inactive. Reality mein bahut zyada states hain jo RevenueCat model karta hai lekin tumhe unke existence ka pata hona chahiye:

  • Active — subscription chal rahi hai, payment successful.
  • Grace Period — renewal payment fail hui (jaise expired card), lekin Apple/Google user ko thoda time dete hain payment method fix karne ke liye — is dauraan access usually maintained rehta hai.
  • Billing Retry — payment provider automatically retry kar raha hai failed payment ko, kai attempts ke through.
  • Cancelled but not expired — user ne cancel kar diya hai lekin already paid period ke end tak access milta rehta hai (jaise "cancelled, expires on 15th").
  • Expired/Refunded — access revoke ho chuka hai.

RevenueCat ye saari states apne CustomerInfo object mein expose karta hai — apna paywall UI aur access-control logic design karte waqt in states ko consider karo, sirf ek simple boolean pe mat rely karo.

⚠️ Currency/Pricing Territory Differences

Ek hi subscription tier har App Store territory mein alag price pe dikh sakta hai (currency conversion, local tax/VAT rules, Apple/Google ke apne pricing tiers ki wajah se). Kabhi bhi price ko hardcode mat karo apne app ke UI mein — hamesha pkg.product.priceString jaisa localized value use karo jo RevenueCat/StoreKit khud provide karta hai, jo already user ke region ke hisaab se correctly formatted hota hai.

⚠️ Webhooks Ke Bina Backend Out-of-Sync Ho Sakta Hai

Agar tumhara app band hai (user ne force-close kiya, ya phone off hai) jab ek subscription renew hoti hai ya cancel hoti hai, tumhara backend ko is event ka pata kaise chalega? App-side listener (addCustomerInfoUpdateListener) tabhi kaam karta hai jab app open ho. Isliye production setup mein RevenueCat webhooks (ya Stripe webhooks, real-world payments ke liye) configure karna zaroori hai — ye tumhare backend ke ek endpoint ko directly hit karte hain jab bhi koi subscription/payment event hota hai (renewal, cancellation, refund, billing failure), chahe app open ho ya nahi. Backend phir apne database mein user ka entitlement status independently update kar sakta hai — isse tumhara server-side data hamesha accurate rehta hai, sirf client ke "jab app khule tab sync ho jaayega" pe depend nahi karna padta.

8. Advanced Deep-Dive — Beyond the Basics

Ab tak humne "kaise integrate karein" cover kiya. Ye section ek level upar jaata hai — ek senior RN developer se expect kiya jaata hai ki wo sirf SDK calls na jaane, balki poori backend architecture samjhe jo real production payment systems ko reliable banati hai. Ye teen concepts — entitlement caching, subscription state machines, aur proration — interview mein sabse zyada puche jaane wale "senior-level" payment topics hain.

8.1 — Server-Side Entitlement Caching Architecture

Section 3 mein humne kaha "backend ko client ke claim pe trust nahi karna chahiye, khud verify karo". Lekin har request pe Apple/Google ke servers ko directly hit karna (jaise ek naya API call jab bhi koi user premium content maangta hai) do problems create karta hai — latency (extra network hop har request pe) aur rate limits (Apple/Google ke verification APIs pe bhi limits hote hain, agar tumhare paas lakhs users hain toh ye scale nahi karega). Senior-level solution: webhooks ko source of truth banao, aur ek local cache maintain karo.

Apple App Store Server Notifications v2 aur Google Real-Time Developer Notifications (RTDN) — dono ka concept same hai: jab bhi koi subscription event hota hai (renewal, cancellation, refund, billing failure, grace period start/end), Apple/Google tumhare configured backend endpoint ko ek signed HTTP POST bhejte hain. Tumhara backend is webhook ko receive karke apne database mein user ka entitlement record update karta hai — aur baad mein jab bhi koi request "kya ye user premium hai?" poochti hai, tum seedha apne fast local database se read karte ho, Apple/Google ko dobara hit kiye bina.

🚨 Danger — Webhooks Pe "Blindly" Depend Karna Bhi Ek Trap Hai

Yahan ek subtle lekin senior-level insight hai jo bahut log miss karte hain: webhooks guaranteed delivery nahi dete. Tumhara server down ho sakta hai jab webhook aata hai, network partition ho sakta hai, ya Apple/Google ke side pe hi koi transient issue ho sakta hai — matlab ek webhook event permanently miss ho sakta hai agar tumne sirf "wait for webhook" architecture banaya. Agar tumhara backend endpoint ek ghante ke liye down raha (deploy, crash, whatever) aur usi dauraan koi refund/cancellation webhook aaya, wo event khoya ja sakta hai — aur ek user jisne refund le liya, uska access forever active reh jaayega jab tak koi manually notice na kare.

Sahi production architecture is failure mode ko accept karti hai aur reconciliation ke through resilient banti hai — matlab webhooks fast-path hain, lekin ek periodic background job (jaise har 6-12 ghante mein cron/scheduled function) bhi chalta hai jo directly App Store Server API / Google Play Developer API ko poll karke actual subscription status verify karta hai aur agar cache stale mila (webhook miss ho gaya tha), use correct kar deta hai. Webhook = fast update, reconciliation poll = safety net. Dono chahiye, sirf ek nahi.

app/api/webhooks/app-store+api.ts (backend)
import { verifyAndDecodeNotification } from "../../../lib/appStoreServerLibrary"; // Apple ka signed JWT payload verify/decode karne wala helper (App Store Server Library se banaya)
import { updateEntitlementCache } from "../../../lib/entitlements"; // hamara apna DB-write helper jo cache table update karta hai

export async function POST(request: Request) {
  const body = await request.json(); // Apple ka POST body — ek signed JWS (JSON Web Signature) payload hota hai

  let payload;
  try {
    payload = await verifyAndDecodeNotification(body.signedPayload); // CRITICAL: signature verify karo — warna koi bhi fake POST bhej ke entitlement forge kar sakta hai
  } catch (err) {
    console.error("Invalid App Store notification signature", err); // signature invalid = untrusted sender, reject karo
    return Response.json({ error: "invalid signature" }, { status: 400 }); // 400 = bad request, Apple ko batao ye payload accept nahi hua
  }

  const notificationType = payload.notificationType; // jaise "DID_RENEW", "EXPIRED", "REFUND", "DID_FAIL_TO_RENEW"
  const subtype = payload.subtype; // additional context, jaise "GRACE_PERIOD" ya "BILLING_RETRY"
  const transactionInfo = payload.data.signedTransactionInfo; // nested signed transaction info — appUserID/productId/expiresDate yahan se milta hai

  // idempotency check — same webhook Apple kabhi kabhi retry/duplicate bhi bhej sakta hai (at-least-once delivery)
  const alreadyProcessed = await hasProcessedNotification(payload.notificationUUID); // apna DB check — is UUID ko pehle process kiya hai kya
  if (alreadyProcessed) {
    return Response.json({ ok: true }); // silently 200 return karo — duplicate hai, dobara process mat karo (double-crediting jaisa bug avoid)
  }

  // entitlement cache table update — yehi wo "fast read" table hai jo har API request use karegi
  await updateEntitlementCache({
    appUserId: transactionInfo.appAccountToken, // hamara internal user ID, purchase ke time associate kiya gaya tha
    productId: transactionInfo.productId, // kaunsa subscription tier/plan
    status: mapNotificationToStatus(notificationType, subtype), // helper jo Apple ke type/subtype ko humare state machine states mein map karta hai (section 8.2)
    expiresAt: new Date(transactionInfo.expiresDate), // exact expiry timestamp — grace period calculations ke liye zaroori
    rawNotificationType: notificationType, // debugging/audit trail ke liye raw value bhi store karo
  });

  await markNotificationProcessed(payload.notificationUUID); // idempotency record save kiya taaki future duplicates skip ho sakein

  return Response.json({ ok: true }); // Apple ko 200 OK — agar ye response na mile toh Apple retry karega (at-least-once guarantee)
}
🧠 Senior Dev Note — 200 OK Fast Return Karo, Heavy Kaam Async Karo

Apple/Google webhook endpoints se fast response expect karte hain (usually few seconds ke andar) — agar timeout ho gaya, wo retry karte hain, jisse duplicate processing risk badh jaata hai. Production pattern: webhook handler sirf payload verify + validate karke ek queue (jaise a database job table, ya SQS/Cloud Tasks) mein push kare, aur turant 200 return kar de. Actual heavy processing (DB writes, downstream notifications, analytics) queue consumer mein async hota hai. Isse tumhara webhook endpoint kabhi bhi Apple/Google ko slow response nahi deta, chahe tumhara downstream logic kitna bhi complex ho.

8.2 — Subscription State Machine

Section 7 (gotchas) mein humne states list ki thi. Ab senior-level insight — ye states ko ek boolean (isActive: true/false) ki tarah treat karna bug-prone hai, kyunki real-world transitions non-obvious hain. Sahi approach ek explicit state machine model karna hai jahan har state se sirf specific dusre states mein transition allowed hai, aur har state ka access-control implication clearly defined hai.

StateUser ko access milta hai?Kaise trigger hota hai
Trial Haan User ne free trial start kiya, abhi tak charge nahi hua
Active Haan Payment successful, subscription current period ke andar hai
Grace Period Haan (ye critical hai) Renewal payment fail hua, lekin Apple/Google user ko payment fix karne ka time de rahe hain (typically 16 din tak, configurable)
Billing Retry Depends (product decision) — often haan grace period ke andar Store automatically failed payment ko retry kar raha hai background mein
Cancelled (period-end pending) Haan, jab tak current period khatam na ho User ne cancel kiya, lekin already-paid period abhi baaki hai
Expired Nahi Grace period bhi khatam ho gaya, ya cancel ke baad period end pahunch gaya
Refunded Nahi (turant) Apple/Google ne refund process kiya (chargeback ya support request)
🚨 Danger — Grace Period Mein Access Cut Karna Sabse Common Real-World Bug Hai

Ye ek bahut real production incident pattern hai: ek naive implementation sirf webhook ka DID_FAIL_TO_RENEW event dekh ke turant isActive = false set kar deta hai aur user ka access cut kar deta hai. Lekin Apple/Google ka poora point grace period ka ye hai ki failed payment ke baad bhi wo automatically retry karte rehte hain (expired card, insufficient funds jaisi temporary issues ke liye) — is dauraan agar tum access already cut kar dete ho, tum ek paying customer (jiska card sirf 2 din baad successfully charge ho jaayega) ko galat tarike se lock out kar dete ho. Ye customer support tickets aur churn dono badhata hai. Sahi logic: GRACE_PERIOD subtype ko apni state machine mein ek separate access-granting state ki tarah treat karo, sirf pure "expired" ya "billing retry period bhi exhaust ho gaya" pe hi access cut karo.

lib/entitlements.ts — state machine helper
// har state ka explicit "does this grant access" mapping — ye single source of truth hai poore backend ke liye
type SubscriptionState =
  | "trial" // free trial period chal raha hai
  | "active" // normal paid, current period ke andar
  | "grace_period" // payment fail hua lekin store retry kar raha hai, access abhi bhi milna chahiye
  | "billing_retry" // grace period ke baad bhi retry chal raha, product decision pe depend karta hai
  | "cancelled_pending" // cancel kiya lekin period end tak access active
  | "expired" // access khatam
  | "refunded"; // access turant khatam, money wapas gaya

// yehi ek jagah hai jahan "kaunsa state access deta hai" decide hota hai — baaki poora codebase isi function ko call karega
export function grantsAccess(state: SubscriptionState): boolean {
  return ( // in states mein access true, baaki sab false — grace_period yahan explicitly included hai (upar wale danger callout ka fix)
    state === "trial" ||
    state === "active" ||
    state === "grace_period" ||
    state === "cancelled_pending"
  );
}

// Apple ke raw notification type/subtype ko humare clean internal state mein translate karta hai
export function mapNotificationToStatus(type: string, subtype?: string): SubscriptionState {
  if (type === "DID_FAIL_TO_RENEW" && subtype === "GRACE_PERIOD") return "grace_period"; // payment fail hua par Apple abhi retry kar raha hai — access rakho
  if (type === "DID_FAIL_TO_RENEW") return "billing_retry"; // grace period nahi hai, pure retry state
  if (type === "EXPIRED") return "expired"; // sab retries exhaust, access cut karo
  if (type === "REFUND") return "refunded"; // turant access cut, money wapas gaya
  if (type === "DID_CHANGE_RENEWAL_STATUS" && subtype === "AUTO_RENEW_DISABLED") return "cancelled_pending"; // cancel kiya, period end tak access
  return "active"; // default — DID_RENEW jaise successful events yahan aate hain
}

8.3 — Proration Handling

Jab ek user apna subscription plan mid-cycle change karta hai (jaise Monthly se Annual pe upgrade, ya ek higher tier se lower tier pe downgrade), Apple/Google/Stripe automatically proration calculate karte hain — matlab remaining unused time ka credit ya extra charge ka fair adjustment. Important senior-level fact: ye behavior platform-specific hai aur tumhare control mein nahi hai — tum proration ka exact formula customize nahi kar sakte, sirf iske around apni UI messaging design kar sakte ho.

  • Apple (StoreKit) — upgrade turant effective hota hai aur remaining time ka prorated credit naye plan ki cost mein apply hota hai. Downgrade turant nahi hota — wo current period ke end tak wait karta hai, phir naya (cheaper) plan agle cycle se start hota hai. Ye asymmetry (upgrade immediate, downgrade delayed) bahut users ko confuse karti hai agar UI mein clearly explain na kiya jaaye.
  • Google (Play Billing) — multiple proration modes support karta hai jo developer choose kar sakta hai (jaise "immediate with time proration", "immediate without proration", "deferred") — Apple se zyada flexibility deta hai, lekin iska matlab tumhe explicitly decide karna padta hai kaunsa mode chahiye per use-case.
  • Stripe (real-world payments/subscriptions) — proration behavior configurable hai (proration_behavior param — create_prorations, none, ya always_invoice), aur tum invoice preview API se exact prorated amount pehle se calculate karke user ko dikha sakte ho checkout se pehle — ye Apple/Google ke comparison mein bahut zyada transparent/controllable hai.
🧠 Senior Dev Note — UI Messaging Hi Tumhara Actual Control Point Hai

Kyunki proration ka underlying math platform decide karta hai, senior-level product/engineering decision ye hota hai ki apni UI copy ko is asymmetry ke hisaab se likho — jaise upgrade button pe "Upgrade now — charged immediately with credit for remaining days" aur downgrade button pe "Downgrade will take effect on your next billing date (DD Mon)" jaisi clear, specific messaging dikhana. Ye avoid karta hai support tickets jaise "maine downgrade kiya lekin abhi bhi purane plan ka paisa kata" — jo actually galat billing nahi hai, sirf expected platform behavior hai jo user ko explain nahi kiya gaya tha.

9. 🎯 Interview Questions — Senior React Native Developer

Ye section un sawaalon ko cover karta hai jo senior/staff-level RN interviews mein payments/monetization ke baare mein poochhe jaate hain — architecture, policy trade-offs, security reasoning, aur real-world debugging scenarios. Answers ek strong senior engineer ki depth se likhe gaye hain, sirf "correct" nahi balki "why" bhi explain karte hue.

Q1. Design the backend data model and flow for handling subscription renewals reliably, including what happens if your webhook endpoint is down for an hour.

Core design: ek subscriptions table jisme columns hon — user_id, product_id, state (state machine se, section 8.2), expires_at, store (apple/google/stripe), last_verified_at. Flow do parallel paths se chalta hai:

  1. Fast path (webhook) — App Store Server Notification/RTDN aata hai, signature verify hoti hai, entitlement cache turant update hota hai. Ye 99% cases mein seconds ke andar sync ho jaata hai.
  2. Safety net (reconciliation job) — ek scheduled job (jaise har 6 ghante) un subscriptions ko pick karta hai jinka last_verified_at purana hai ya jo expires_at ke close hain, aur directly App Store Server API/Play Developer API se unka current status verify karta hai.

Agar webhook endpoint ek ghante ke liye down raha — us dauraan aaye events lost ho sakte hain (Apple kuch retries karta hai, lekin infinite nahi). Isliye reconciliation job hi actual safety guarantee deta hai: jab endpoint wapas up ho, agla scheduled reconciliation run automatically stale records ko detect karke correct kar dega, chahe koi specific webhook miss ho gaya ho. Ye design "eventually consistent, never permanently wrong" guarantee deta hai — jo ek pure webhook-only design nahi de sakta.

Q2. Explain exactly why Netflix removed the ability to subscribe from within their iOS app — what's the underlying App Store policy and business trade-off?

Streaming subscription Apple ki policy ke hisaab se ek digital good maana jaata hai (Category A, section 1) — matlab agar Netflix apne app ke andar StoreKit se subscription becha, Apple 15-30% commission le sakta hai us revenue pe. Netflix jaisi company ke liye, jiska subscriber base already crores mein hai, ye commission absolute dollar terms mein bahut bada hit hai. Business trade-off: Netflix ne apne app se in-app-purchase flow hi hata diya — naye users ko app ke andar subscribe karne ka option hi nahi milta, unhe bahar (netflix.com, browser mein) bhej diya jaata hai subscribe karne ke liye. Isse Apple ka commission poori tarah avoid ho jaata hai, kyunki subscription transaction Apple ke ecosystem ke bahar hoti hai. Trade-off cost: thoda sa conversion friction (user ko app se bahar jaana padta hai), lekin at Netflix's scale ye friction cost commission-saving se kaafi kam hai. Ye pattern "reader apps" ke liye Apple ne explicitly ek External Purchase Link Entitlement ke through bhi formalize kiya hai (kuch categories mein, regulatory pressure — especially EU DMA — ke baad).

Q3. Why is validating a purchase receipt server-side non-negotiable for a subscription-gated feature, even though the client SDK already tells you "purchase succeeded"?

Client-side "success" signal sirf ye batata hai ki uss specific device pe StoreKit/Billing API ne success return kiya — ye cryptographically kuch bhi prove nahi karta backend ke liye. Attack surface: jailbroken/rooted devices pe tools exist karte hain jo fake success responses inject kar sakte hain, reverse-engineered/modified app builds client-side boolean ko directly true set kar sakte hain, aur network-level tampering (agar verification hi client-only hai) response modify kar sakta hai. Receipt (iOS) ya purchase token (Android) ek cryptographically signed proof hai jo sirf Apple/Google generate kar sakte hain — server-side is receipt ko unke verification API se check karna hi actual "was this real" guarantee deta hai. Bina is step ke, tumhara premium-gated backend endpoint effectively unauthenticated hai as far as payment status is concerned — koi bhi client apna request bas "I'm premium" claim karke bhej sakta hai.

Q4. A user says they paid but don't have access — walk through your debugging process across app, backend, and store dashboard.

  1. Get identifiers first — user ka account ID/email, aur agar possible unka Apple/Google transaction ID ya order ID (screenshot se, ya support ticket se).
  2. Check store dashboard — App Store Connect/Play Console mein us transaction ko search karo. Confirm: payment actually settled hua ya pending/failed reh gaya (jaise "payment declined but user saw a confusing UI that looked like success").
  3. Check RevenueCat/webhook logs — RevenueCat dashboard (ya apne webhook logs) mein dekho — kya event tumhare backend tak pahunchi thi? Agar RevenueCat mein entitlement active dikh raha hai lekin apne DB mein nahi, iska matlab webhook miss hua ya process nahi hua — reconciliation job manually trigger karo.
  4. Check your database's entitlement cache — kya subscriptions table mein us user ka correct state hai? Agar nahi, ye backend-side bug hai (webhook handler mein koi silent failure, ya idempotency check galti se duplicate ko drop kar raha).
  5. Check client-side — kya app khud stale cached state dikha raha hai (jaise app background mein tha jab entitlement update hua, aur listener trigger nahi hua)? Force refresh/restart se resolve ho sakta hai.
  6. Cross-platform mismatch check — kya user ne ek platform pe purchase kiya (jaise iOS) aur doosre pe check kar raha hai (jaise Android web)? Agar appUserID/account linking sahi se set nahi hai, entitlements merge nahi honge.

Root cause identify hone ke baad — agar genuinely backend bug hai, manually entitlement grant karo (support tooling se) aur underlying bug (missed webhook, race condition, waghera) ko fix karo taaki future users affected na hon.

Q5. What's the difference between how you'd model "grace period" access versus a simple isActive boolean, and why does this distinction matter at scale?

Ek boolean model sirf do states allow karta hai — true/false. Real subscription lifecycle mein (section 8.2) grace period, billing retry, cancelled-pending jaise states hain jinme access should still be granted temporarily. Agar tum boolean use karte ho aur "payment failed" event pe seedha false set karte ho, tum paying customers (jo temporary card issue ke wajah se fail hue, aur jinhe Apple/Google khud hi retry kar rahe hain) ko incorrectly lock out kar dete ho. At scale — agar tumhare paas lakhs subscribers hain aur even 2-3% payments temporarily fail hote hain renewal date pe (common, expired cards ki wajah se), ye ek significant chunk of paying users ko wrongly cut kar dega, jo support tickets aur churn dono spike karega. Ek explicit state machine (enum ke saath, boolean nahi) is nuance ko naturally capture karta hai aur access-control logic ko ek single grantsAccess(state) function mein centralize karta hai (jaisa 8.2 code mein dikhaya), jisse consistency guarantee hoti hai poore codebase mein.

Q6. Why can't a client directly create a Stripe PaymentIntent with the amount, and what specific attack does the backend-creates-intent pattern prevent?

Agar client apna khud ka amount specify kar sake PaymentIntent creation mein, ek attacker apna app traffic intercept/modify kar sakta hai (proxy tool se) ya reverse-engineered build bana sakta hai jisme wo request body mein amount ko manipulate kare — jaise ek ₹10,000 ka order ₹1 amount ke saath submit karna. Chunki PaymentIntent creation ke liye Stripe secret key chahiye (jo sirf server pe honi chahiye), aur secret-key-holding backend hi authoritative amount decide karta hai apne database se (order/cart lookup se, client input se nahi), client ke paas amount tamper karne ka koi rasta nahi bachta. Ye exact same "never trust client input for anything that affects money/security" principle hai jo ch.9 ke RLS/backend-validation discussion mein bhi aata hai.

Q7. How would you test subscription renewal, grace period, and cancellation flows without waiting real weeks/months for them to naturally occur?

Apple ka Sandbox environment specifically isi problem ko solve karta hai — sandbox subscriptions accelerated speed pe renew hoti hain (jaise ek monthly subscription sandbox mein ~5 minutes mein renew ho jaati hai, weekly kuch minutes mein), taaki tum multiple renewal cycles, grace period transitions, aur expiry ko ek testing session ke andar hi observe kar sako. Google's License Testing similarly accelerated renewal simulate karta hai. Production-grade testing approach: ek dedicated Sandbox Tester Apple ID (kabhi real ID use mat karo), ek test card jo Sandbox mein deliberately decline ho (Apple specific test cards provide karta hai jo grace period trigger karte hain), aur apne webhook handler ko in accelerated events ke against test karna — taaki tumhara state machine (Q5) aur reconciliation job (Q1) dono verify ho sakein bina real production users affect kiye.

Q8. If RevenueCat (or your IAP provider) has an outage, what's your fallback strategy so paying users aren't locked out?

Ye ek availability/resilience question hai jo senior interviews mein often aata hai. Sahi answer: apna backend entitlement cache (section 8.1) hi tumhara resilience layer hai — agar RevenueCat down hai, tumhara app RevenueCat ko live query nahi karta har baar, balki apne locally-cached entitlement record pe depend karta hai jo pichhle successful sync se already stored hai. Is design mein RevenueCat outage ka impact sirf naye purchases/renewals ka real-time sync delay hota hai, na ki existing users ka access loss — kyunki unka cached state already valid hai aur expiry date tak honor hota hai. Additionally, client-side bhi ek short grace-tolerance rakhna sensible hai (jaise agar entitlement check fail ho network issue ki wajah se, turant lock mat karo, last-known-good state pe thodi der trust karo) — isse transient outages user-facing impact nahi banti.

Q9. Walk through the security reasoning for why raw card number input (a plain TextInput) is a fundamentally different risk profile than Stripe's CardField component.

Jab card number ek plain TextInput mein type hota hai aur tum use apne JS state mein store karte ho (chahe temporarily) aur apne backend ko bhejte ho, raw card data tumhare app runtime aur backend infrastructure ke through guzarta hai — jiska matlab wo poora surface (device memory, network layer, tumhara server, tumhara logging agar accidentally log ho gaya) ab PCI-DSS scope mein aa jaata hai, jisme heavy requirements hote hain (encryption-at-rest, network segmentation, regular penetration testing, audits). CardField is problem ko completely bypass karta hai — card input Stripe ke apne native, sandboxed SDK code ke andar capture hota hai aur seedha tokenize hoke Stripe ke servers ko jaata hai; raw number kabhi tumhare JS runtime ki memory mein bhi nahi aata as a plain string tumhare control mein. Isi wajah se tumhara business lightest compliance tier (SAQ A) mein reh sakta hai — jaisa section 5 mein cover kiya.

Q10. Explain the "restore purchases" requirement — what specific bug does it prevent, and why do so many apps get rejected for missing it?

Non-consumable purchases aur subscriptions ek Apple ID/Google account ke against register hote hain, na ki ek specific app install ke against. Agar user apna app delete karke reinstall kare, ya ek naya device le, unka purchase history unke Apple ID/Google account mein already exist karta hai — lekin tumhara app (naya install hone ki wajah se) ye nahi jaanta ki unhone pehle se pay kiya hua hai, jab tak explicitly check na kiya jaaye. Bina restore-purchases option ke, aisa user apna already-paid subscription/feature dobara access nahi kar paayega bina dobara pay kiye — jo Apple ki guideline ke against hai (unfair double-charging risk) aur ek bahut poor user experience hai. Yehi wajah hai ye Apple review guidelines mein explicitly mandatory hai aur missing hone pe apps consistently reject hoti hain — ye ek "easy to forget jab MVP jaldi ship karna ho" requirement hai, isliye senior devs isse checklist mein explicitly rakhte hain (jaisa section 7 mein discuss kiya).
